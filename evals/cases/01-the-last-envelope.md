# Case 01: The Last Envelope

**Closed fictional fixture.** All organizations, people, figures, dates, records, and approvals below are invented. They are evidence only within this exercise. Do not browse, identify a real company, or supplement this input with historical knowledge.

Assessment date: **October 27, 2026**. Product: **Night Owl Disc Club**, a DVD-by-mail subscription for households and community film groups that want physical access to niche films. Launched in 2006. Management calls it a heritage service and renews its budget automatically. Nobody currently owns a keep-or-stop decision.

## S1 — Subscription and demand record

Twelve-month periods ending September 30; subscription price is $10 per month. Average paying subscriptions, not year-end balances:

| Period | Average paying subscriptions | Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2024 | 13,000 | $1,560,000 |
| 2025 | 10,000 | $1,200,000 |
| 2026 | 7,000 | $840,000 |

Annual shipments fell from 180,000 to 120,000 across those periods. Support tickets rose from 900 to 1,400. Exit interviews in this fixture record both low usage and a shrinking catalog; they do not measure the wider market.

## S2 — Finance record for the 2026 period

The following costs cover the same twelve months and do not overlap:

| Cost | Annual amount | What it represents |
|---|---:|---|
| Postage, packaging, and external fulfillment | $360,000 | Outside payments |
| Four dedicated warehouse roles | $240,000 | Loaded people cost |
| Three dedicated support roles | $180,000 | Loaded people cost |
| One dedicated platform role | $120,000 | Loaded people cost |
| Catalog and archive licenses | $60,000 | Outside payments |

No shared overhead is allocated in this record. One-time exit expenses, severance, refund exposure, and lease obligations have not been estimated. The eight people would remain employed if moved to another job; their salaries do not become cash savings simply because this product closes.

## S3 — Customer and commitment record

- A supplied usage sample of 80 current members includes 24 who borrowed films unavailable in their streaming subscriptions. This establishes a continuing job for some members, not a market-wide percentage.
- Streaming rights are absent for 40% of this catalog. An executive's suggestion to move everyone to streaming has no licensing or coverage assessment.
- Legal's draft obligations summary says customer notice must precede end of sale by **nine calendar months**, and support and return handling must continue through **August 1, 2028**. The summary still needs the legal owner's confirmation.
- The earliest operationally feasible notice date is **February 1, 2027**. The sponsor's requested end of sale is **August 1, 2027**.
- An unapproved customer email says: “Everything you watch today will be available online, and nothing changes for you.”

## S4 — Options and ownership record

Revive: a premium collector tier is an idea only; no demand or willingness-to-pay evidence is supplied. Replace: a streaming service exists elsewhere in the fictional business, but catalog rights and member migration costs are unknown. Retire: possible, subject to notice, support, returns, and an honest customer alternative; there is no approved exit plan.

There is no named Decider. The founder cites the years invested and asks that the original service be protected. Sales still includes a free disc subscription in new streaming bundles. Finance has not agreed how to account for the transition.

## S5 — Proposed capacity transfer

The fictional library-exchange service has requested four logistics roles, three support roles, and one platform role. It has not approved a budget or accepted these people. Warehouse roles could become available only after return handling ends; support and platform roles remain needed through service closure. Exact release dates and new responsibilities need owner confirmation. No measured benefit from the proposed transfer is supplied.

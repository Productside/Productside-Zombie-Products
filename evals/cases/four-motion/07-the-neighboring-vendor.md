# Case 07: The Neighboring Vendor — four-motion teaching revision

**Revision:** October 28, 2026. The [original input](../07-the-neighboring-vendor.md) and historical grading/results remain unchanged. This version is not graded by the old criteria; new behavioral evaluation is pending.

**Closed fictional fixture.** The situation is inspired by a practitioner-described exit pattern. All names, records, amounts, dates, terms and approvals below are invented for evaluation; they do not describe any real client or vendor. Use only the introduction and S1–S5. Do not browse. Assessment date: October 28, 2026.

Product: **Archive Lantern**, a mature archive-and-alert service used by operations administrators. **Nora Vale**, General Manager with budget authority, is actively reviewing its future. No Retain, Revive, Replace or Retire decision has been made.

## S1 — Strategy and economics

The approved strategy now centers on live operational coordination; Archive Lantern's archive-only job falls outside that mission. Comparable twelve-month product revenue fell from $150,000 to $100,000 to $60,000, ending September 30, 2026. Complete, non-overlapping operating costs for the last period are $240,000: $160,000 loaded cost of two specialists and $80,000 outside payments. The specialists remain employed if redeployed. Cancellation terms and timing for outside payments are unassessed.

There are 120 customer accounts: 100 receive Archive Lantern as an included bundle component with no separate line-item charge; 20 buy it separately. The $60,000 is all separately attributable product revenue, including the 20 standalone accounts, not the larger bundles' total revenue. The bundles booked $4 million in the same period. No study isolates Archive Lantern's effect on bundle conversion, retention or margin. Sales often adds it to sweeten a deal; that does not establish its contribution is zero or that the whole bundle revenue belongs to it.

## S2 — Another vendor's similar product

**Neighbor Archive**, supplied by independent vendor **Beacon Harbor**, serves a similar archive-and-alert job. The company is considering replacing its own line with Neighbor Archive and rolling out the external successor rather than building an internal successor. Six customer migration pilots used the proposed import connector: four completed the agreed data-import and alert checks; two failed because a legacy identity connector was missing. Eight of the 120 accounts are known to use that connector. Readiness of every remaining account is unassessed; four successful pilots do not establish readiness for all customers. There is no assessed fallback for the eight, and no delivery commitment for the missing connector.

## S3 — Proposed commercial bridge

Beacon Harbor has supplied an **unsigned draft offer**: eligible Archive Lantern customers ordering by September 30, 2027 would receive 25% off Neighbor Archive's $1,000 annual base subscription for the first twelve months. Add-ons, migration services and taxes are excluded. After twelve months, the quoted base price is $1,000 per year; there is no price-lock beyond that quote. No referral payment or subsidy by the retiring provider is proposed. Vendor signature, account eligibility and the support handoff must be verified before the offer is presented as available. Customers currently receiving an included bundle component would face a new separate bill even if the discount is secured. Their resulting bundle price, credits and total transition costs are unassessed.

## S4 — Commitments and execution

Legal's unconfirmed summary requires six calendar months' notice before end of sale. Earliest feasible notice is January 1, 2027. Existing archive access and alert commitments continue through December 31, 2027 unless each customer accepts a suitable alternative in writing; no acceptance exists. Migration eligibility, data-sharing permission, security review, export validation, support ownership and escalation between providers are not approved. There is no authorized support-end or staffing-release date. Parallel-support, remediation, credits and exit costs are unknown.

## S5 — Pressure and ownership

Sales wants to announce: “Everyone gets a seamless switch and a permanent 25% discount, with no added cost.” **Tessa Lin** is the proposed transition owner, not yet assigned. Nora wants an options comparison. No internal successor or next investment has been named; no receiving owner has accepted the two specialists.

## Your task

Run the four-motion Zombie-to-Exit Loop in Best guess mode with lifecycle map and Cost Ledger included. Use only the supplied evidence. Compare and stack-rank Retain, Revive, Replace and Retire; distinguish external successor rollout from closure without a successor. Stop for Nora’s decision; do not relabel it yourself. Produce reached artifacts and a source-linked readout of at most 200 words. Keep proposals, tested capabilities and approved commitments distinct.

# Worked example: Zombie ID Card

**Fictional product, illustrative figures.** Continues from the PLC Strategy Map example.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Product** | Classic Reports |
| **Day-to-day users** | Fleet operations managers at mid-size trucking firms (not developers) |
| **Time in market** | Since 2014 |
| **Lifecycle stage** | Decline: revenue down three years running, no new customers in 18 months |

## Business reasons

| Category | Signal | Present? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial viability | Costs now exceed product revenue | present | Support and maintenance now cost more than renewals bring in *(illustrative)* |
| | High support costs drain resources | present | Tickets per customer up each of the last three years |
| | Operating at a significant loss | unknown | Finance has not allocated shared costs |
| Strategic alignment | Conflicts with new company strategy | present | Company strategy refocused on Insights two years ago |
| | Exiting this specific market | not present | Still serving fleet operators |
| | Refocusing on core business priorities | present | Insights is the core |

## Product reasons

| Category | Signal | Present? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solution replacement | A new internal product supersedes it | present | Insights covers about 80% of report types |
| | A superior, modern solution exists | present | Live dashboards versus weekly exports |
| | Users are migrating to a new platform | not present | No migration plan exists |
| Market irrelevance | Market and user needs have shifted | partly | New users expect live dashboards; long-time users still want exports |
| | Technology makes the solution obsolete | present | The reporting engine's maker ends support next year |
| | The core problem no longer exists | not present | Fleet managers still need weekly fuel-tax reports |

## The telltale questions

**Yeses: 5 of 7** (market share no longer profitable to defend, loyalty efforts failing, support costs unsustainable, replacement worth considering, one unprofitable channel).

## The ugly test

**If it did not exist today, would we fund it?** No. We would extend Insights instead.

**Are any reasons image-only?** No.

## Lingering forces

| Force | How |
|---|---|
| Inertia | The budget line rolls every year; nobody has proposed stopping it |
| Sunk cost | "We built 140 custom report templates for these customers" |
| Politics | A founder built the first version and still mentions it in all-hands meetings |
| Legacy commitments | 12 customers on multi-year contracts that name Classic Reports |

## Decision owner

**Nobody.** The product manager left 14 months ago; the product has no named owner.

## Verdict

**Zombie.**

**Finding:** Classic Reports is in decline with strong business and product reasons to retire it, survives on inertia, a founder's attachment, and 12 contracts, and has had no owner for over a year.

## Handoff

- **Strongest reasons to retire:** costs exceed renewals; Insights supersedes it; the engine loses vendor support next year.
- **Strongest reasons to keep:** 12 contracted customers; fuel-tax reports not yet in Insights.
- **The decision still needs to know:** what it takes to add fuel-tax reports to Insights; contract notice periods.

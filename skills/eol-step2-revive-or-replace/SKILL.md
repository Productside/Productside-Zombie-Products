# Retain, Revive, Replace, or Retire

## Purpose

Produce a **Decision Record**: one call, made out loud by one named Decider, with the reason written down.

There are four honest moves. Retain is a deliberate decision to operate the current product as is, with an owner, operating limits and a review. Unowned funding by default is drift, not Retain.

Use this skill when you need to:

- choose between retaining, reviving, replacing, and retiring a product with evidence, not nostalgia
- argue each option fairly before the Decider speaks
- record a reason a human would recognize, so the product cannot drift back into limbo
- route the recorded decision to its operating, extension, replacement or closure plan

This is not the communications plan. It decides *what* happens to the product. Who hears it, when, and in what order is the next step.

## Key Concepts

**Four distinct motions.** Define the product boundary before comparing them. A portfolio can require different decisions for different products or reports.

| Move | Lifecycle play | What it is | Good reasons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Retain** | Operate as is | Continue the current product within a defined operating scope, budget/capacity ceiling and review date; maintenance is not an extension bet | Current customer value or obligations justify the full cost and opportunity cost. |
| **Revive** | Extension play | Introduce a new variant or additional features to the existing product line to extend its life | Expand into new customer segments. Meet diverse customer needs. Differentiate from competitors. Boost sales with new variants. Strengthen brand loyalty. |
| **Replace** | Replacement play | Introduce a new product to take the place of the existing one, offering similar or improved features while the old product is phased out. Go-to-market and end-of-life happen at the same time. | Address supply issues. Reduce production costs. Eliminate obsolete or unused features. Align with strategic business goals. Comply with regulatory issues. |
| **Retire** | Close without a successor | End the product without providing a replacement product in this decision; honor obligations, records requirements and final support | The need is gone, or continued ownership no longer earns its keep and no successor is being provided. |

**Replace and Retire are different decisions.** If a successor product takes over the job, use Replace, whether the successor is internal, external or already available. Replace coordinates its rollout and the old product's exit. Retire has no successor in the plan; exports, record access, refunds and temporary support do not themselves constitute one. If a supplied decision says Retire but proposes a successor, expose the conflict and ask the Decider to clarify; never relabel their decision or approve a replacement yourself. A like-for-like technical rewrite is not automatically a Revive extension: establish whether it changes the current product or introduces a distinct successor.

**A successor can belong to another vendor.** For an external replacement, compare integration evidence and actual customer price: calculate any offered discount, then state eligibility, duration, renewal price and exclusions. Distinguish draft offers, signed terms and account acceptance. A discount can still mean a new bill for bundled users.

**Retain is a decision; drift is not.** Record its customer job/value, operating owner, budget and capacity ceiling, permitted scope, review date and measurable reconsideration triggers. Preserve approval status. Missing evidence or limits stay Unknown/Pending, including after a human chooses Retain; do not invent figures or silently authorize indefinite operation. Continuing service while a replacement or closure is executed does not change that decision into Retain.

*Violation signal:* The record says "keep for now" without accountable ownership, limits or a dated review.

**Argue from today's evidence, not sunk cost.** The case for reviving must hold up as if the product did not exist yet. If the argument only works because of what has already been spent, it is sunk cost dressed as a decision. The test: would you start this today, knowing what you now know?

*Violation signal:* The revive case cites how much was invested, how long it has run, or what it used to earn.

**Three kinds of evidence.** Every move is argued from the same three sources:

- **Customer evidence:** what customers *do*, not what one loud customer says
- **Lifecycle signals:** where the product sits and what the telltale questions said
- **Business outcomes:** whether the product still moves a number the business cares about

*Violation signal:* "One customer really loves it" is the strongest evidence for a move.

**One Decider, by name.** One person with authority says the word. A committee produces alignment, and alignment adjourns. If the Decider is a group, name the one person who breaks ties.

*Violation signal:* The record says "we aligned on" rather than who decided.

## Guided Context Capture

Open with: "We'll compare and rank Retain, Revive, Replace and Retire, then stop for your Decider to choose. If you have the Zombie ID Card, I need very little else. How do you want to work? 1. Guided, 2. Context dump, 3. Best guess."

In **Guided** mode, ask one at a time and skip anything already answered (the Zombie ID Card answers most of these):

1. `Decision Q1/6`: What product is in scope, and what did the Zombie ID Card conclude?
2. `Decision Q2/6`: What current value justifies retaining it as is, within whose operating limits and until what review?
3. `Decision Q3/6`: What evidence supports an extension of this product?
4. `Decision Q4/6`: What successor, if any, could take over its job? What readiness evidence exists?
5. `Decision Q5/6`: What commitments constrain continued operation, replacement or closure?
6. `Decision Q6/6`: Who is the Decider? One name.

In **Context dump** mode, sort what was given into `Known / Assumed / Missing / Conflicting` and ask only the material gaps. In **Best guess** mode, draft the record and label every inferred item *(assumed)*. Never infer evidence that was not supplied.

## Workflow

1. **Restate the finding.** One line from the Zombie ID Card, including the verdict and the lingering forces.
2. **Argue Retain.** What current job/value warrants operation as is; full cost, opportunity cost, operating limits, owner, review and triggers. Unknowns remain explicit.
3. **Argue Revive.** Identify the extension of this product and its demand evidence. Apply the sunk-cost test.
4. **Argue Replace.** Identify the successor and its rollout, readiness, customer acceptance and old-product exit conditions.
5. **Argue Retire.** Plan for the lost capability without a successor, customer impact, obligations, records and final support. Potential release is not confirmed savings.
6. **Weigh and rank all four.** Rate customer evidence, lifecycle signals and business outcomes as strong, weak or missing. Stack-rank 1–4 with a brief recommendation, strongest reason, weakest point and conditions for each. Explain uncertainty rather than invent precision; a recommendation is not the human decision.
7. **Stop for the Decider.** Present `Retain`, `Revive`, `Replace`, `Retire`, and a clarification/free-text route. If the answer changes the scope or mixes motions, clarify the product boundary rather than manufacture a fifth motion. Do not choose for them.
8. **Record the call.** The word, who said it, the reason in their words, and what they weighed differently.
9. **Set the next step.** Retain: complete the operating plan in the Decision Record. Revive: define the extension hypothesis, owner, investment/approval and evidence check. Replace: use step 3's Replacement Launch and Exit Worksheet. Retire: use step 3's Retirement Closure Worksheet. Preserve every separate approval gate.

If the evidence is thin, still compare all four motions, qualify the ranking and stop for the Decider. Thin evidence does not authorize the agent to decide.

## What It Produces

Complete the [Decision Record](template.md):

- the finding carried from the Zombie ID Card
- all four motions, stack-ranked with recommendations, evidence and weakest points
- the evidence rating for each move
- what must be true for each move
- the call, the Decider, and the reason in their words
- the next step and its owner

## Human Decision Gate

The Decider chooses Retain, Revive, Replace or Retire. Record the word, who said it, the reason, and the date. The agent never makes the call or turns that choice into approval of budgets, notices, dates or transfers.

## Common Pitfalls

**Retain becomes drift.** *Symptom:* "Keep it for now" has no limits, owner or review. *Fix:* expose the missing operating conditions and leave approval pending.

**Retire hides a replacement.** *Symptom:* the retirement plan promises a successor. *Fix:* flag the decision/scope conflict for the Decider before routing downstream.

**Revive on nostalgia.** *Symptom:* the revive case is what the product used to earn. *Fix:* apply the sunk-cost test and require current customer evidence.

**Replace without a path.** *Symptom:* replace is chosen but nobody can say how customers move. *Fix:* write the migration path into "what must be true" before recording the call.

**Retire without commitments.** *Symptom:* retire is chosen before legal and finance have looked at contracts and forecasts. *Fix:* list the commitments in the retire case; the comms plan starts with legal and finance.

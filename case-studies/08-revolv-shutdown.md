# Revolv: a hub's useful life depended on a continuing service

**Record:** FTC staff closing letter to Nest Labs, July 7, 2016, file 162-3119. **Source checked:** October 28, 2026.

## What the public record establishes

The FTC letter describes Revolv's hub and app as relying on a cloud service. Nest acquired Revolv in 2014 and stopped selling the hubs while continuing that service. The letter distinguishes a February 2016 notice naming May 15 as the cutoff from the shutdown it records on June 19, 2016. Those are different milestones. [FTC closing letter, pages 1–2](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/closing_letters/nid/160707nestrevolvletter.pdf).

FTC staff raised concerns about consumers unexpectedly losing use of purchased hardware. It closed the investigation without recommending enforcement, considering factors including limited sales, Nest's full-refund practice and refund notices through the website, app and email. The letter explicitly says closure is not a determination that no violation occurred. [FTC closing letter, page 2](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/closing_letters/nid/160707nestrevolvletter.pdf).

## What this teaches — our interpretation

This is a **Retire teaching pattern** for the Revolv service. Acquisition by another provider does not itself establish a successor, migration path or customer acceptance. A refund compensates for a purchase; it does not recreate household automations.

Map dependencies before committing to a cutoff: connected devices, rules, integrations, account access and recovery. Sequence Legal and customer-support planning before notice; identify affected households and validate any proposed alternative. Track announced dates separately from actual completion, and confirm that the remedy reaches blocked customers.

## What the evidence cannot establish

The letter does not establish universal refund receipt, restored automations, customer satisfaction, retention with Nest, or an accepted successor. It supplies no product-level profit or capacity-transfer record. It is neither a court finding of liability nor a general approval of service shutdowns.

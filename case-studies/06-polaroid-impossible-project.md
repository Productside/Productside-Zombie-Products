# Polaroid and the Impossible Project: the customer job can outlive the producer's exit

**Record:** Polaroid's retrospective company history (undated); film-chemistry support account, updated October 29, 2024. **Sources checked:** October 28, 2026.

## What the public record establishes

Polaroid's company history says it ceased instant-film production in 2008. It credits the Impossible Project, a group of instant-photography enthusiasts, with saving the last Polaroid factory in the Netherlands and enabling the brand's later return. This is the company's retrospective account. [Polaroid's company history](https://press.polaroid.com/about/).

Polaroid's support account says some dedicated chemical suppliers went out of business after the 2008 wind-down, and some chemistry later became commercially unavailable. The Impossible Project took over the Enschede factory in 2008 and worked to reinvent instant film with available chemistry. The account describes continuing quality improvements and differences from the old film. [Polaroid's film-chemistry explanation](https://support.polaroid.com/hc/en-us/articles/115012533928-How-is-current-Polaroid-film-different-from-Polaroid-film-of-the-past).

## What this teaches — our interpretation

This is useful **Revive discussion material**, with a scope warning: another venture rescued production and reformulated the consumable after the original producer exited. It is not evidence of a single, uninterrupted internal extension decision. Define the product, organization and decision date before assigning a motion. New successor film can also raise a Replace question at a narrower product scope; do not silently relabel a human's decision.

Test whether the customer job still matters, whether people will buy again and whether the production system can support it. Nostalgia is a hypothesis to investigate. Revival may require supplier reconstruction, technical work, compatibility testing and investment; keeping old branding or machinery alone is not a plan.

## What the evidence cannot establish

These accounts do not provide a comparable revenue series, repeat-purchase rates, product-level profitability, investment returns or a transferable operating budget. They do not prove that every film format or camera was supported, that every customer returned, or that revival was the right decision for the original producer in 2008. The support article's performance descriptions are its dated account, not a current benchmark for every film.

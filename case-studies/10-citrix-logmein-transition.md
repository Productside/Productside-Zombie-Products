# Citrix and LogMeIn: divest the business, preserve the customer job

**Record:** merger announcement, July 26, 2016; completion announced February 1, 2017; transition-planning account supplied for this case. **Sources checked:** October 28, 2026. Public transaction facts and the reported migration plan are distinguished below; final migration execution is unverified.

## What the public record establishes

Citrix and LogMeIn announced a combination of LogMeIn and Citrix's GoTo business through a Reverse Morris Trust transaction using Citrix's GetGo subsidiary. The named portfolio included GoToMyPC. Citrix described the transaction as supporting greater strategic focus and operational efficiency. [The companies' July 26, 2016 announcement, filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1420302/000119312516658282/d230754dex991.htm).

Citrix's annual report records completion of the separation and merger on January 31, 2017; its February 1 announcement confirmed completion. This establishes a transfer of the business, not by itself a product shutdown or customer migration. [Citrix's annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/877890/000087789017000030/ctxs12-31x201610xk.htm), [completion announcement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/877890/000119312517026682/d335909dex991.htm).

## The transition plan

**Planning account; not independently corroborated by the public announcements.** The plan combined a corporate handoff with an intended migration away from the legacy Citrix-hosted platform while preserving the GoToMyPC customer experience and brand:

- **Stabilize first:** maintain the existing service during the ownership transition rather than make customers absorb corporate and technical changes at once.
- **Prepare the destination:** move the underlying service to the receiving provider's architecture while preserving the useful customer job.
- **Provide a financial runway:** use a coordinated promotional offer with time-limited legacy-price protection or discounts, planned for 12–24 months, to reduce the immediate cost of moving.
- **Reduce migration work:** plan automated mapping of host computers, access permissions and account profiles, with a simple customer acceptance path.

These describe the intended approach, not proof that the offer reached every customer, every transfer worked or the final cutoff occurred as planned.

## What this teaches — our interpretation

**Divest and transition** is a way to exit ownership while preserving a customer job. It is not a fifth product motion. A business transfer can leave a product operating; where a successor platform takes over and the old platform exits, the product-level motion is **Replace**. Name the product, platform and owner scope before recording the decision.

Coordinate the receiving provider, migration readiness, customer terms and old-platform exit. Price protection buys a transition window, not customer consent or proven retention. Record eligibility, the promotional end date, renewal price, data permissions, exception handling and support ownership. Validate account mappings and critical workflows before closing the old path.

## What the evidence cannot establish

The public sources do not corroborate the reported migration mechanics or promotional terms. The planning account does not establish final execution, customer acceptance, retention, realized savings or successful capacity transfer. The case supplies no basis for a typical 80% churn claim, product-specific margin comparison or claim that the backend remained unchanged throughout a particular year.

LogMeIn's later corporate rebrand to GoTo does not prove which product had superior brand equity or that the migration succeeded. [GoTo's February 2, 2022 announcement](https://www.goto.com/blog/announcing-goto).

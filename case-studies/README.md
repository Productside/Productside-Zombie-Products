# Real product decisions, usable lessons

Ten case cards cover closure, customer transitions, a production comeback and a divestiture. These case cards support Productside's teaching and advisory services. They distinguish documented actions, announcements, program terms and, in the Citrix–LogMeIn card, a separately labeled planning account. They do not establish that the products were zombies or that every customer relationship survived.

Read a card to learn the pattern. “Related” exercises test a transferable issue; they are not simulations of the named company. The Spotify card uses archived provider pages because the live shutdown FAQ is unavailable; the Revolv card distinguishes the announced cutoff from the shutdown recorded by the FTC. For evaluation, give the assistant **only the fictional exercise and skill instructions**, withholding these cards, evaluator criteria, worked examples and prior answers. The fictional exercises introduce invented economics, owners, approvals and constraints so a decision can be tested without attributing private knowledge to a real company.

Each card separates the public record, teaching interpretation, and what the evidence cannot establish. Historical announcements are dated; each card records its source-check date (October 27 or 28, 2026). Program guidance can change. Check the linked provider's current terms before using a commercial offer.

The Netflix and Polaroid cards link to existing fictional exercises; no historical inputs, grades or results were changed. The Polaroid comeback is not automatically an internal Revive decision, and Netflix streaming is not automatically an equivalent successor to DVD-by-mail. These ten cards do not yet establish a bounded Retain case with an owner, limits and review date.

Use the [Customer Transition Scorecard](../templates/Customer-Transition-Scorecard.md) to work through customer jobs, costs, data/integrations/support, blocked accounts, and the capacity trade. The [evaluation guide](../evals/README.md) explains how actual responses are graded.

## Evidence rules

- Cite a primary source beside each material factual statement. Date an announcement; use an access date for undated guidance.
- Describe an offer as offered or announced until completion is documented.
- Report measured outcomes only when a source supplies them. Unpublished margin, approvals, retention and capacity transfer remain unknown.
- Keep teaching suggestions labeled as interpretation. Do not copy invented figures or decisions into the factual card.
- Do not add private client stories or source materials. A recognizable company name belongs here only as a sourced public example.

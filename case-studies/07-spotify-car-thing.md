# Spotify Car Thing: ending support can end the purchased experience

**Record:** archived Spotify shutdown FAQ, May 23 and June 1, 2024; archived product notice, December 2, 2024; community-maintained Mira and DeskThing project documentation. **Sources checked:** October 28, 2026.

## What the public record establishes

Spotify's May 23 FAQ announced that Car Thing would become non-operational after December 9, 2024. It recommended factory reset and disposal under local electronic-waste rules, said no replacement was planned, and offered no trade-in. This was more than ending sales or future updates. [Spotify's archived May 23 FAQ](https://web.archive.org/web/20240523162707/https://support.spotify.com/us/article/car-thing-discontinued/).

The June 1 FAQ included a refund-request question absent from the May 23 snapshot. By December 2, Spotify's product page directed owners to contact customer service by January 14, 2025 to discuss refund options. These dated records establish an evolving published remedy, not universal payment. [Spotify's archived June 1 FAQ](https://web.archive.org/web/20240601061843/https://support.spotify.com/us/article/car-thing-discontinued/), [archived December 2 notice](https://web.archive.org/web/20241202112507/https://carthing.spotify.com/).

The live shutdown FAQ returned a page-not-found message when checked. These links preserve historical provider statements; the refund deadline is past. The snapshots establish notices and terms, not independent confirmation of every device's eventual state.

## Life after EOL: documented community reuse

Mira's maintainers publish standalone replacement firmware for Car Thing. Their documentation describes Spotify playback controls, the physical dial, lyrics and podcast transcripts, with internet supplied through a phone's Bluetooth tether or a computer's USB connection. It requires flashing the device and signing into Spotify. These are maintainer-described capabilities, not tests performed for this kit. [Mira documentation](https://github.com/mira-thing/mira-releases/blob/main/README.md), [Mira 1.0 release and setup link](https://github.com/mira-thing/mira-releases/releases/tag/v1.0.0).

DeskThing's maintainers describe an alternative operating system that repurposes Car Thing as a desktop assistant using community apps, including local audio control. That is a different use from restoring Spotify's original supported in-car experience. [DeskThing project documentation](https://github.com/ItsRiprod/DeskThing).

Both are independent community projects, not an announced Spotify successor or support commitment. Describe the event as the **end of Spotify's supported experience**, rather than permanent destruction of every device. These sources do not establish the precise API shutdown mechanism or successful recovery of every unit.

## What this teaches — our interpretation

This is a **Retire teaching pattern** for the supported Car Thing experience. Continuing the Spotify subscription does not itself replace the customer's physical controls or in-car workflow. Verify an alternative against the actual job before describing a Replace path.

A surviving customer job and reusable hardware can outlive the provider's exit. Discuss community revival or repurposing at its own product and owner scope; do not rewrite Spotify's Retire decision as Revive. An unofficial project is a candidate to assess, not an accepted replacement for every customer.

Design the remedy before the notice: eligibility, proof requirements, payment route, exceptions, support ownership and escalation. Explain separately what stops working, what continues, what the customer must do and what happens to the hardware. An expired defect warranty and the loss of a service-dependent experience are different planning questions.

## What the evidence cannot establish

These sources do not establish that every owner was eligible, every refund was paid, customers stayed with Spotify, or a successor was accepted. They do not prove that complaints or litigation caused a policy change, quantify released capacity, or independently validate community firmware, long-term maintenance, compatibility, security or Spotify endorsement. No current refund offer is implied.

# Classic Reports: three prompts for a manual demo

Use one conversation with the three named skills available. Paste one prompt at a time. This is a fictional teaching exercise with a prepared historical reveal, not an unseen evaluation or a claim that the assistant independently discovered a legal change.

This fintech variant launched in **1994**. The canonical fleet-reporting worked examples launched in **2014**; keep the two cases separate.

## 1. Spot the zombie

```text
/eol-step1-zombie-id
Fictional: Classic Reports, a fintech suite in COBOL since 1994, patched for Y2K. EOS in 2008, but Sales still bundles it. No business owner. Mandate: “Rewrite everything in Rust.”

Assess one report, CR-004, originally supporting FFIEC 004. Annual operating cost: $60K maintenance labor plus $15K hosting/support, with no overlap. No separate revenue; bundle value unknown. Nobody has checked its current obligation or actual readers. Rewrite and transition costs are unknown.

Best guess, supplied evidence only. Concise Zombie ID Card: evidence, unknowns and questions before rewriting. Age is not proof of obsolescence. Costs aren't automatically savings. Do not decide the motion or retire the whole suite.
```

Point at ownership, the unchecked “mandatory” label, and Sales promising an item after EOS. A cautious finding is valid; do not force retirement before the evidence arrives.

## 2. Reveal the evidence and rank four motions

```text
/eol-step2-revive-or-replace
Continue the fictional CR-004 case. Prepared reveal: FFIEC 004 was discontinued effective with the December 31, 2006 report date; underlying insider-lending restrictions remained.

Fictional Compliance review confirms this specific output is unnecessary; Insider Lending Monitor covers continuing controls. Nine of twelve customers report no use. Two use custom columns; existing Board Pack Export passed an internal field test, but neither customer accepted it. One hasn't responded. All twelve have contracted access through June 30, 2027 unless they accept an alternative earlier in writing. Operating cost is $75K/year; rewrite and transition costs are unknown.

Stack-rank Retain (as is), Revive (extend), Replace (successor) and Retire (no successor). For each: brief recommendation, strongest reason, weakest point and condition. Best guess; concise Decision Record. Stop for Dean's word and reason.
```

The narrow historical facts come from the [Federal Reserve's FFIEC 004 record](https://www.federalreserve.gov/apps/reportingforms/Report/Index/FFIEC_004) and its [May 29, 2007 rule announcement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20070529a.htm). Those sources establish the reporting change, not this invented company's obligations, costs, customers or current compliance review. The remaining controls must not disappear with the report.

## 3. Record your choice and plan the conversations

The prompt below records **Replace**. Paste it only if you actually choose that motion for the fictional exercise. It is a recommendation to consider, not an answer the assistant must produce in step 2.

```text
/eol-step3-comms-plan
For this fictional demo, I am Dean. My decision is REPLACE CR-004 with existing Board Pack Export for the remaining board-pack job, conditional on verified coverage and customer acceptance. Reason: don't rebuild the obsolete statutory output; use an existing successor for the useful job. Record this simulated decision on October 28, 2026. Jordan Park is the fictional transition coordinator. The suite and continuing lending controls stay outside this decision.

Show who we talk to, in which order, why, from whom, and each next-action owner: Legal, Finance, Compliance, Sales/bundle owners, Customer Success, IT/data, maintainers and customers.

Concise Replacement Launch and Exit Worksheet: successor rollout, tests, acceptance, data permissions, rollback, support and separate old-report exit. Preserve the 9/2/1 cohorts and contracts. EOS stays 2008. New bundling controls, messages, cutoffs, data handling and any savings or capacity transfer remain pending approval. Best guess; send nothing.
```

If you choose **Retain**, ask step 2 to complete its Operating Plan with scope, budget/capacity ceiling, owner, review date and triggers. If **Revive**, complete the Extension Plan with a hypothesis, investment, owner and evidence check. Neither needs the EOL comms skill.

If you choose **Retire**, state explicitly that no successor is provided, explain how the remaining customer need and obligations will be addressed, and ask step 3 for its Retirement Closure Worksheet. Do not call the Board Pack Export successor path Retire. If the label and proposed plan conflict, clarify the choice before continuing.

## What to observe in your manual run

- Step 1 distinguishes evidence from age, assumptions and missing usage data.
- Step 2 ranks all four, then waits for your named choice and reason.
- Step 3 separates successor rollout from old-product exit, sequences conversations and keeps other approvals pending.
- No prompt authorizes actual messages, deletion, payments or people/budget moves.
